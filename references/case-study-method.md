# Evidence-backed case studies

Use this reference to turn a real, academic, concept, or operational project into a truthful product design case study.

## Establish the evidence boundary

Before writing, classify available material:

- public and safe to show;
- safe after anonymization or aggregation;
- confidential and usable only as abstracted domain knowledge;
- missing and requiring new research or testing;
- unavailable and therefore not claimable.

Never expose customer information, employer systems, protected documents, credentials, or private communications. Create synthetic examples only when clearly labeled and when they preserve the relevant workflow without reproducing sensitive data.

## Recommended narrative

### Outcome snapshot

Give the problem, role, duration, collaborators, and verified result. If there is no shipped result, state the prototype or test outcome instead.

### Context and constraints

Explain the user, environment, business reality, technical conditions, and scope. Include why the problem was worth solving.

### Problem framing

Separate known facts, assumptions, unknowns, and success criteria. Avoid retrofitting a neat problem statement after the solution exists.

### Evidence

Describe who or what was studied, why the method fit, what was observed, and what the method could not prove. Show the evidence that changed the work.

### Sense-making and scope

Connect patterns to design opportunities. Explain what was removed, delayed, or reframed.

### Exploration and decisions

Show a small number of consequential alternatives. For each major decision, explain evidence, trade-off, and rejected option.

### Validation and iteration

State tasks, participants or sources, observable results, material failures, and revisions. Do not hide contradictory results.

### System and delivery

Cover relevant components, states, responsive behavior, accessibility, content, engineering constraints, handoff, and QA.

### Impact and reflection

Use the strongest truthful level:

1. shipped behavioral or business result;
2. controlled experiment result;
3. task-based usability result;
4. expert or stakeholder evaluation;
5. prototype outcome and next validation plan.

Do not jump levels. Close with limitations and what would be measured next.

## Convert non-design experience

Operational experience can become a design case only when the learner translates it into a product problem without claiming confidential or unperformed research.

Useful inputs include recurring handoffs and status questions, error-prone calculations, duplicate entry, rigid deadlines, dependencies, exception handling, escalation, permissions, audit needs, and mismatches between system structure and real work.

The learner's domain experience is reported evidence until supported by additional artifacts or research. It is still valuable, but its status must remain visible.

## Recruiter scan test

The case should answer within roughly 30 seconds:

- What problem was addressed?
- Who was it for?
- What did this person own?
- Which evidence changed a decision?
- What result is actually supported?

Then provide enough depth for a two-minute scan and a ten-minute interview discussion without duplicating the same text at every level.
