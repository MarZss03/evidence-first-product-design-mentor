# Project studio

Use this reference to guide an end-to-end product design project. Move forward when the current decision has enough evidence for the project's risk; do not demand heavyweight research from a low-risk exercise.

## Stage 1: Frame

Produce the user, task, context, desired outcome, constraints, non-goals, evidence ledger, riskiest assumption, and first observable success condition.

Gate: the project is framed as a user or system problem, not merely a feature request.

## Stage 2: Investigate

Choose the lightest method capable of reducing the riskiest uncertainty. Options include interviews, contextual inquiry, artifact review, support-log analysis, analytics, competitive task comparison, or a domain-expert walkthrough.

Produce the research question, source rationale, raw observations separated from interpretation, patterns, contradictions, limitations, and decisions the evidence changed.

Gate: findings can be traced to actual observations or clearly labeled reported evidence.

## Stage 3: Model the system

Depending on the problem, produce a task flow, journey, service blueprint, object model, state model, or permissions map. Include relevant failure paths, handoffs, deadlines, dependencies, and recovery routes.

Gate: the model explains both the happy path and the most consequential exception.

## Stage 4: Explore and choose

Generate meaningfully different approaches, not cosmetic variants. Compare them against user value, evidence, operational fit, accessibility, technical cost, and reversibility.

Produce a decision record containing the chosen direction, a rejected alternative, the accepted trade-off, and the assumption still unresolved.

Gate: the learner can explain why the chosen approach is preferable without relying on taste.

## Stage 5: Prototype the risk

Prototype the uncertain interaction or system behavior before polishing the entire product. Cover enough fidelity to test the hypothesis and include relevant loading, empty, error, permission, destructive, and completion states.

Gate: the prototype can produce observable success or failure for the primary task.

## Stage 6: Validate and revise

Define tasks, success signals, observation notes, issue severity, and stopping conditions before the session when practical.

Produce an evidence table by task, prioritized issues, revisions linked to each material issue, before-and-after rationale, and unresolved limitations.

Gate: at least one meaningful decision is supported, changed, or rejected because of evidence.

## Stage 7: System, handoff, and reflection

Document applicable tokens, components, states, responsive behavior, accessibility, content rules, analytics needs, and technical constraints.

Finish with what shipped or was tested, what the evidence supports, what remains unknown, and the next learning step.

Do not present a prototype as production, usability feedback as commercial impact, or a design system screenshot as proof of implementation quality.
